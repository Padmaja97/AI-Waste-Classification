04literature

What 25 papers left open.

Five gaps came out of the review. One is closed by this build, one is partly closed, and three are the roadmap. Click a gap to open it.

  1. Paper #30 classifies organic vs recyclable only (2 classes). Paper #10 handles e-waste only (77 classes). Nothing merges the two into one decision surface.

    Not addressed here. This build is the 2-class half only. The 77-class e-waste dataset was selected during the literature review but no model was trained on it, so no claim is made about e-waste performance.

    refs #30 · #10 · #2

  2. Benchmarks come from UK, Ireland and Japan. Indian MSW runs near 60% organic and carries items the Western sets do not contain — coconut shells, jute, mixed sachet plastics. Papers #31 and #32 study Indian streams but without any vision model.

    Not addressed here. The Kaggle set used for training is not Indian-sourced, so accuracy on Indian street waste is unmeasured.

    refs #31 · #32 · #21

  3. YOLOv11 in paper #10 needs a GPU. The 1 MB CNN in paper #30 only reaches 80.9%. Neither end is deployable-and-accurate.

    What this build did: fine-tuned MobileNetV2 — figures load from the evaluation report.

    Caveat: — No on-device (Android/iOS) deployment has been built or benchmarked yet.

    refs #10 · #30 · #2

  4. Every CNN and YOLO paper in the set is a black box. None uses Grad-CAM, SHAP or LIME — a problem the moment a municipal operator has to trust an automated bin.

    What this build did: Grad-CAM hooked on the final convolutional block, rendered live in the inspection bay above, so every prediction ships with the evidence behind it.

    refs #30 · #10 · #26

  5. Paper #10 proposes an edge architecture but never builds it. Paper #5 puts medical waste on a blockchain with no vision layer. No end-to-end smart bin exists in the set.

    Not addressed here. This is a web demo on a laptop, not a deployed bin.

    refs #10 · #5 · #26

05SBM rules

Which bin, and why.

Swachh Bharat Mission fixes the colours nationally: green takes wet biodegradable waste, blue takes dry recyclables. The model's two classes map onto them.

The lists below are the municipal rules for people, not model outputs. EcoSort predicts only organic or recyclable — it does not recognise these items individually.

Green bin

Wet · biodegradable

Collected daily · composted or bio-methanated

  • Kitchen and vegetable scraps
  • Fruit peels, egg shells
  • Used tea leaves and coffee grounds
  • Cooked food waste, leftovers
  • Garden trimmings, flowers, leaves
  • Coconut shells and husk

Rule of thumb: if it rots within weeks, it is green.

Blue bin

Dry · recyclable

Collected on alternate days · sorted at MRF

  • PET bottles and rigid plastics
  • Paper, newspaper, cardboard
  • Glass bottles and jars
  • Aluminium cans, metal tins
  • Tetra Pak cartons (rinsed)
  • Clean cloth and packaging

Rule of thumb: rinse it, dry it, then it is blue.

Red / separate

Hazardous

Separate collection · authorised recycler

  • Batteries (AA, lithium, car)
  • E-waste: phones, chargers, cables
  • Tube lights, CFLs, LEDs
  • Paints, solvents, pesticides
  • Medicines, syringes, thermometers
  • Motor oil, aerosol cans

Rule of thumb: if it can leak, burn, or corrode, it is hazardous.

Black bin

Non-Recyclable

Landfill / incineration · minimise this stream

  • Soiled or multi-layer packaging
  • Diapers and sanitary products
  • Broken ceramics, mirrors
  • Used shoes, worn-out textiles
  • Thermocol and styrofoam
  • Mixed-material sachets

Rule of thumb: if no bin above takes it, it is black.

EcoSort classifies into four categories but cannot identify individual items within each. Always verify hazardous items before disposal — incorrect routing of batteries or chemicals is a safety risk.
